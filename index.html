About Me

I'm a Robotics Engineering and Sociology student interested in human-robot interaction, artificial intelligence, and the social and ethical implications of intelligent systems. I enjoy exploring robotics and artificial intelligence through multiple disciplinary perspectives, specifically robotics, sociology, and philosophy, to understand how people interact with these technologies and how they should be governed.

I'm especially interested in how robots influence human decisions, how people respond to robots in social settings, and how these systems can be designed and governed responsibly.

OREGON STATE UNIVERSITY REU: ROBOTS IN THE REAL WORLD

Push It (Real Good): Examining Human Choices to Engage Robots in Group Interaction

Completed

Led multiple pilot studies investigating the research question: In what ways are robots actively included in group conversations, and what factors shape people’s willingness to do so? Built custom ROS-based code enabling Quori to listen, transcribe, and respond to participants through button-press interactions, while logging responses and behaviors. Tested Quori under three social stance conditions (Empathic, Logic/Cold, and Frustrated) to examine differences in engagement. Collected quantitative measures (button press counts, speaking time, participant engagement) and qualitative observations (tone of interaction, trust, and inclusion). Future phases will expand this work using validated surveys (RoSAS, GAToRS) to assess attitudes toward robots, trust, and social presence.

UNDERGRADUATE RESEARCH

“‘AI can’t write like that!’: A Test of People's Ability to Differentiate Between Human and AI-Generated Text"

Published

Examined how four different writing styles influences readers' ability to distinguish human-written text from AI-generated text across different contexts, and what this suggests about how AI is changing our cultural understanding of human authorship and originality.
